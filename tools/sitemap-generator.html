    What this tool does
    
      
    
        
    
          A sitemap is a plain XML file that lists the URLs on your site so a crawler can find them
          without having to guess its way through your navigation. This tool takes a list of URLs and
          produces a valid one: the right XML declaration and namespace, one url entry
          per address, and the optional lastmod, changefreq and
          priority elements written the way the protocol expects them.
        
    
        
    
          It also does the unglamorous part that actually breaks sitemaps: rejecting lines that are not
          absolute URLs instead of silently writing them, escaping ampersands in query strings so the
          file stays well-formed XML, and removing duplicates. The honest limitation is that it has no
          idea what your site contains — it does not crawl anything, so you supply the URLs, and it
          cannot tell you whether any of them actually returns a 200. A sitemap full of 404s or
          redirects is worse than no sitemap.
        
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    How to use it
    
      
      
        
    1. Paste your URLs into the box, one per line. Fragments are stripped and duplicates are dropped for you.
      2. 
        
    2. Set a last modified date if the pages genuinely changed on one date. If they did not, leave it empty — a wrong lastmod teaches the crawler to distrust the whole file.
      3. 
        
    3. Choose a change frequency and priority only if you have a reason to. Google ignores both, so "Omit" is usually the better answer.
      4. 
        
    4. Click Generate Sitemap, fix any lines that were skipped, then copy the XML and save it as sitemap.xml at your site root.

    Common use cases
    
      
      
        
    • 
          
      A new site with no internal links yet
      
          
      On a fresh domain nothing points at your pages, so a sitemap is often the fastest way to get them discovered at all.
      
        
      • 
        
    • 
          
      Orphan pages
      
          
      Landing pages and campaign URLs that nothing links to will not be crawled on their own — listing them is the only reliable way in.
      
        
      • 
        
    • 
          
      Large catalogues and archives
      
          
      When you have thousands of product or post URLs, a generated sitemap beats hoping the crawler reaches page 40 of your pagination.
      
        
      • 
        
    • 
          
      After a migration
      
          
      Building the list from your new URL structure is a quick way to spot addresses you forgot to redirect.
      
        
      • 
        
    • 
          
      Client handover
      
          
      Generate the file, then hand over a sitemap you can explain line by line rather than one exported from a plugin nobody understands.

    Frequently asked questions
    
      
    
        
    
          Do I need a sitemap to rank?
          
    
            Not strictly. Google discovers pages by following links, and plenty of sites rank well
            without one. A sitemap earns its place on large sites, on pages that are poorly linked
            internally, and on a new domain where nothing points at you yet. It tells a crawler what
            exists — it is not a ranking factor, and submitting one will not lift a page that nothing
            links to.
          
    
        
    
        
    
          How many URLs can one sitemap hold?
          
    
            Up to 50,000 URLs, and the uncompressed file must stay under 50 MB. Past that limit you
            need a sitemap index: a small file listing several sitemap files. This is a protocol rule
            rather than a preference — search engines reject a file that exceeds it, which is why the
            tool warns before you get there.
          
    
        
    
        
    
          Do changefreq and priority affect ranking?
          
    
            Almost never. Google has said plainly that it ignores both, deciding when to recrawl from
            its own scheduling and from lastmod. They remain valid protocol elements that
            other crawlers may read, so setting them is harmless — but leaving them out is cleaner than
            publishing values you cannot back up.
