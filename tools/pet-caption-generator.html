Pet Caption Generator

A described photo and a caption to go with it. Filter for dogs or cats, or leave it on both — the two pools are drawn separately.

Words only. This writes a caption and describes a photo. It does not make or find an image, and nothing is uploaded.

The photo

What this tool does

Two pools — sixteen described photos and sixteen captions — both tagged for dogs or cats and both filtered by the animal selector. The two are drawn independently, so the scene and the line are not matched to each other. That mismatch is usually where the joke is: a caption about shameless behaviour over a photo of a dog waiting politely by a door works better than a tidy pair.

Neither pool contains a photograph. Every scene is a sentence describing one, which means the output is usable with whatever picture you actually have of your own animal, and nothing is ever uploaded. The dog and cat pools are written in different registers rather than swapped nouns, because a caption that works on a dog usually does not work on a cat. Dogs get enthusiasm and no interior life; cats get entitlement and a formal register.

How to use it

  1. Choose an animal — dogs, cats, or both.
  2. Press Generate a caption. A described photo and a caption appear together.
  3. Press again for a new pair. Both pools rotate through the current filter before repeating anything.
  4. Press Copy to take the caption, or Clear to empty the result and reset the pools.

Common use cases

  • Captioning a photo of your own pet

    The described scene tells you which of your photos fits, and the line is short enough to paste straight in.

  • Pet accounts

    Sixteen captions per animal is a reasonable backlog, and the register is right for the format.

  • Group chats

    Short, no edge on them, and nothing that depends on knowing the animal.

  • A photo you cannot think of a caption for

    Press the button three times. Sixteen scenes and sixteen captions cross into far more combinations than there are photos of your pet.

Frequently asked questions

Does this make or find an image?

No. It describes a photo in words and writes a caption. There is no image generation, no search and no upload. You use it with a picture you already have.

Why do the photo and the caption not match?

They are drawn from separate pools on purpose. The mismatch is usually funnier than a matched pair, and it means the caption pool stays usable no matter what your actual photo is.

Are the dog and cat captions different?

Yes. They are written as two separate pools in two different registers rather than one pool with the animal name swapped. Dog captions are enthusiastic and uncomplicated; cat captions are formal and entitled.

Can I use these for a pet account?

Yes. The output is yours, there is no attribution requirement, and nothing in the pools references a real person, brand or animal.
