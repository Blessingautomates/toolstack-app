Meme Caption Generator

A described image, a top line and a bottom line. Five tones, and the image is drawn from its own pool so the picture and the words are never paired.

Words only. This tool writes the words. It does not make an image, find one, or upload anything — you take the text and put it wherever you were going to put it.

The picture

What this tool does

Three parts, from three separate pools: a described image, a top line and a bottom line. The image pool has no tone and is drawn independently, so the picture and the words are regularly mismatched — which is deliberate, because the mismatch is often the joke. The two line pools are matched to each other by tone, so the top and bottom at least belong to the same register.

The images are described rather than supplied. Every one is a sentence describing a photograph — a cat on a keyboard, a person holding a phone at arm’s length — so the output is usable with whatever picture you actually have, and no image is ever downloaded, generated or stored. The page says this in the widget rather than in the small print, because pressing a button labelled "generate a meme" and getting text is a surprise worth preventing.

How to use it

  1. Choose a tone. The top and bottom lines are drawn from that tone’s pools; the image is tone-independent.
  2. Press Generate a caption. The image description, top line and bottom line appear together.
  3. Press again for a new set. The image bag and the two line bags each track their own repeats, so nothing comes back immediately.
  4. Press Copy to take the whole caption, or Clear to empty the pools and start the rotation again.

Common use cases

  • Captioning a photo you already have

    Take the described image as a prompt for what kind of picture would work, and use the lines on the one you have.

  • Group chats

    The classic and relatable tones are written for this. Both are short enough to paste directly.

  • Work channels

    The work tone is specific rather than generic — it uses the actual phrases, which is what makes it land.

  • Breaking a blank page

    If you know the picture you want to caption but not the words, the bottom line pool is usually the fastest way in.

Frequently asked questions

Does this make an image?

No. It writes text and describes a picture in words. There is no image generation, no image search and no upload. You take the caption and use it with a photo you already have.

Why is the described picture unrelated to the caption?

Because the image pool is drawn separately from the line pools and has no tone attached. The mismatch is intentional — a caption about a meeting over a picture of a pigeon is a better prompt than a tidy match.

Is any of this taken from real memes?

No. The image descriptions and the lines are all written for this page. There are no named templates, no real people and no brands anywhere in the pools.

Can I use the captions commercially?

Yes. The output is yours and there is no attribution requirement. The pictures you pair it with are your responsibility, as they always were.
