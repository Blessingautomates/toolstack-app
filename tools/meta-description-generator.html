    What this tool does

    A meta description is the two lines of grey text under your headline in a Google result. It is not a ranking factor, but it is the copy that decides whether someone clicks your result or the one above it — which makes it the highest-leverage writing on the page for anything already ranking. This tool takes a one-line summary of your page and a target keyword, then composes five variants around them at different lengths and angles.

    The variants are templates filled with your own words, not prose invented by a language model. That is a deliberate constraint: it means the tool can never state a fact about your product that you did not supply. The trade-off is real — a template does not know your page, so treat the output as a strong first draft to edit rather than a finished tag. And remember Google rewrites descriptions it judges unhelpful for a given query, so none of this guarantees the snippet you see.

    How to use it

    1. Enter the primary keyword you want the page to rank for. It is woven into the variants so the snippet bolds the phrase someone searched.
    2. Write one sentence describing what the page actually offers. No full stop, no marketing throat-clearing — just the substance.
    3. Add secondary keywords if you have them, choose a call to action and a tone, then click Generate Descriptions.
    4. Each variant shows its character count and a colour: green inside 120–158, amber when short, rose when it will be truncated.
    5. Click a variant to load it into the snippet preview, then copy the one that reads best — usually the middle length, not the longest.

    Common use cases

    • Rewriting thin snippets

    • Product and service pages

    Frequently asked questions

    How long should a meta description be?

    Aim for 120 to 158 characters. Google truncates the snippet by pixel width at roughly 920 pixels on desktop, which lands near 158 characters for average-width text. Under about 120 characters you are leaving persuasion on the table but nothing breaks; over 158 the end of your sentence gets cut off mid-thought, which reads as careless.

    Does Google always use the meta description I write?

    No. Google uses your description most of the time, but it rewrites the snippet when the query is better answered by text on the page, or when it judges your description low quality or duplicated across many pages. Writing a specific, honest description for every page is the best way to keep control of it — generic ones are the first to be replaced.

    Are the generated descriptions unique, or are they templates?

    They are templates filled with the exact words you supply, not an AI model writing original prose. That is deliberate: it means every sentence is factually something you told the tool, so nothing is invented about your product. The trade-off is that you should still edit the result — a template does not know your page better than you do, and two pages given similar inputs will produce similar output.
