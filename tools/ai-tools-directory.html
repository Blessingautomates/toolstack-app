What this tool does

It is a short, curated index of AI tools that are genuinely usable without paying, organised by what they do rather than by who makes them. Type into the search box and it matches against each tool's description and a set of intent keywords, so searching "paraphrase" finds the rewriting tools and "video" surfaces the video-generation entries even when the word is not in the name. The category chips narrow the list further, and the count above the grid tells you exactly how much you are looking at.

The list is deliberately short. An exhaustive directory of every AI product would be thousands of entries and useless; each of these had to clear three bars — usable on its free tier for real work, actively maintained, and covering something the others do not. Read the free-tier caveat honestly, though: a free tier is an allowance, not a licence to unlimited use. Caps, queues, watermarks and commercial-use restrictions are common, and they change without notice, so confirm the terms on the provider's site before you build a workflow around one.

How to use it

  1. Start with a category chip — Writing, Coding, Image, Video or Audio — to cut the list to the discipline you are working in.
  2. Narrow further by typing in the search box. It searches descriptions and intent keywords, not just names, so describe the task rather than guessing at a product name.
  3. Press Escape in the search box to clear it, or click Clear search & filters if nothing matches.
  4. Click Visit Site on a card to open the tool in a new tab.
  5. Check the provider's pricing page before you commit — the free tier you are relying on may have changed since this list was written.

Frequently asked questions

Are these tools really free?

Every entry has a free tier you can use without paying, but free tiers are allowances rather than unlimited access: they come with usage caps, slower queues, watermarks or commercial-use limits. AI pricing changes constantly, so confirm the current terms on the provider's own site before you build a workflow around one.

How were these 28 tools chosen?

Each entry had to be usable for real work on its free tier, be actively maintained, and cover something the others on the list do not. That is why the directory is short: it is a curated set of tools you can actually start with today, not an exhaustive index of every AI product that exists.

Can I use the output commercially?

It depends entirely on the provider, and the free tier is often where the restriction lives. Some grant full commercial rights on the free plan, others restrict it to personal use or require a paid plan before you can sell what you make. Check the licence of the specific tool before you ship anything commercial.
