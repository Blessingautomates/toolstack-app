Reaction Caption Generator

A described reaction and a single caption line. Five tones, drawn separately — so the image and the words are never matched to each other.

Words only. This tool writes the words. It does not make an image, find one, or upload anything — you take the text and put it wherever you were going to put it.

The reaction

What this tool does

A described reaction image and a single caption line. The difference from the meme caption generator next door is the shape of the output: that one builds a two-line top-and-bottom meme, whereas this is one reaction and one line — the format you use when somebody says something and you need to respond with a picture and three words. Both pools are drawn separately, so the reaction and the caption are not matched to each other, and the resulting mismatch is usually better than a tidy pair.

The reactions are described rather than supplied, so the output works with whatever image you actually have. Five tones cover the useful registers: deadpan for the flat response, hype for genuine enthusiasm, defeated for the specific tiredness of a long week, warm for the ones with no edge on them, and chaotic for everything else.

How to use it

  1. Choose a tone. The caption is drawn from that tone’s pool; the reactions are tone-independent.
  2. Press Generate a reaction. The described reaction and the caption appear together.
  3. Press again for a new pair. Both pools rotate through their contents before repeating anything.
  4. Press Copy to take the reaction and the caption, or Clear to reset the pools.

Common use cases

  • Replying to a message

    One line and a described picture is the whole reply. Short enough to be a reaction rather than a response.

  • Group chats

    The deadpan and chaotic tones are built for this. Neither is mean.

  • Choosing what to post

    The described reaction tells you what kind of image would work, which is useful when you have the line but not the picture.

  • Finding the right register

    If you know the feeling and not the words, cycling the five tones is faster than trying to write it.

Frequently asked questions

Does this create the image?

No. It describes a reaction in words and gives you a caption line. No image is generated, searched for or uploaded. You pair the text with a picture you already have.

Why do the reaction and the caption not match?

They come from separate pools and are not paired. A deadpan caption on an enthusiastic reaction is often funnier than a matched set, and the mismatch gives you more to work with.

What are the tones?

Deadpan, hype, defeated, warm and chaotic. Each has its own caption pool. The reactions themselves have no tone, so any reaction can come up with any tone.

Is anything stored?

No. Everything happens in the page. There is no account, no history and nothing is sent anywhere.
