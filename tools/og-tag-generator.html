What this tool does

      

        

          Open Graph tags are a separate set of meta tags from the ones Google reads. They tell Facebook, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Slack,
          Discord and X what to draw when someone pastes your URL — the headline, the one-line summary, the image, and the site name
          above it. Without them a platform guesses from your page, which usually means no image and a headline pulled from whatever
          text it finds first.
        

        

          This generator assembles the block and previews it as you type, entirely in your browser. Two honest limitations: the
          preview is an approximation rather than a pixel-accurate render of any one platform, and the tool cannot clear a platform's
          cache for you. If you change your tags after a link has already been shared, the old preview will persist until you ask the
          platform to re-scrape the URL.
        

      

    


    

      
How to use it

      
    
        
  1. Write og:title as the headline you want on the card. It can differ from your SEO title — social copy can afford to be more direct.
    2. 
        
  2. Write og:description. Facebook shows about two lines, so front-load the value in the first 100 characters.
    3. 
        
  3. Paste an absolute https image URL. Relative paths silently fail on every platform. 1200×630 is the size that fills the large card layout without cropping.
    4. 
        
  4. Pick the og:type that matches the page — article for blog posts, product for a product page, website for everything else.
    5. 
        
  5. Click Generate OG Tags, copy the block, and paste it into your page head — then re-scrape the URL if it has been shared before.

Frequently asked questions

      

        

          What is the difference between Open Graph tags and regular meta tags?
          

            Regular meta tags describe your page to search engines, which render a text snippet. Open Graph tags describe it to social
            platforms, which render a card with an image. The two are read by different systems, so a page can rank well in Google and
            still produce a blank, image-less preview on Facebook if the og: tags are missing.
            Fill in both.
          

        

        

          Why is my Open Graph image not showing up?
          

            The three usual causes are a relative image URL, an image smaller than 200×200 pixels, and a cached scrape.
            og:image must be an absolute https URL, ideally
            1200×630 and under 5MB. If the tag was added after the link was first shared, force a re-scrape with Facebook's Sharing
            Debugger or LinkedIn's Post Inspector — until then the platform keeps serving the preview it cached.
          

        

        

          How large should an og:image be?
          

            1200×630 pixels is the safe default. That is a 1.91:1 ratio which fills the Facebook, LinkedIn and WhatsApp card layouts
            without cropping, and stays sharp in the large-image layout. Anything under 600×315 usually falls back to a small thumbnail
            beside the text, which is a noticeably weaker share.
