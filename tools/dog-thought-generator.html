Dog Thought Generator

Forty thoughts in one consistent voice — short sentences, present tense, no context — plus an excitement meter that is drawn at random and says so.

What the dog is thinking, as far as anyone can tell. Short sentences, enormous enthusiasm, no context whatsoever.

Current thought

What this tool does

Forty thoughts written in one consistent voice: short sentences, immediate present tense, no reasoning about consequences. That last part is the constraint the whole pool is built on. A dog thought that weighs up an outcome is not a dog thought, so nothing here does — the thoughts are all reaction, repetition and total conviction about something trivial.

The excitement meter is a joke rather than a measurement, and the page says so in the meta line under the result. It is drawn at random on every thought and has no relationship to what the thought says. There is no dog, nothing is being assessed, and a meter that pretended otherwise would be the dishonest version of this page. The cat generator next door is the opposite voice on purpose: long formal sentences that end in a verdict.

How to use it

  1. Press Find out what the dog thinks. A thought and an excitement level appear together.
  2. Press again. Thoughts are drawn from a shuffled bag, so all forty come up before any of them repeat.
  3. Press Copy to take the thought. The meter is not included, because it is not part of the thought.
  4. Press Clear to empty the result and reset the bag.

Common use cases

  • Captioning a photo of a dog

    The thought works as the caption and the photo supplies the context the sentence deliberately lacks.

  • Pet accounts

    Forty thoughts is a long run of posts, and the voice is consistent enough to build a character on.

  • Cheering somebody up

    The register is enthusiastic and entirely harmless, which is the point of it.

  • Comparing with the cat version

    The two pages are written as opposites. Running both is more interesting than running either.

Frequently asked questions

Is the excitement meter real?

No. It is drawn at random every time and has nothing to do with the thought. The page says so under the result rather than only here, because a number presented without that context looks like a measurement.

Can this tell me what my actual dog is thinking?

No. It is a generator with a written pool of forty thoughts. It has no information about your dog or any other dog.

How is this different from the cat thought generator?

The voice. Dog thoughts are short, immediate and enthusiastic; cat thoughts are long, formal, and end in a ruling on the situation. They are two separate pools written to be opposites rather than one pool with the animal changed.

Does anything get saved?

No. Nothing is stored between visits and nothing leaves your browser. Pressing Clear only resets the rotation within the current page.
